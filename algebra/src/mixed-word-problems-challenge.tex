\section*{Instructions}
Allow 30 minutes for Problems 1--9 and 20 additional minutes for Problems
10--12. A basic calculator is allowed. Define variables, write an equation
or inequality, and show your reasoning. Include units and interpret answers
in context; reject any solutions that do not fit the situation. Give exact
answers unless rounding is explicitly requested. Give money amounts to the
nearest cent when needed. Use the space provided or attach extra paper.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
  \item A club makes a display using red and blue tiles in the ratio $3:5$.
  There are 64 tiles altogether.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item How many tiles of each color are there?
    \item The club adds 16 blue tiles and no red tiles. What is the new
    ratio of red tiles to blue tiles, in simplest form?
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A school play sells adult tickets for \$9 and student tickets for
  \$5. A total of 86 tickets brings in \$626. How many tickets of each type
  were sold?
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A club has \$485 for a field trip. Transportation costs a flat
  \$125, and admission costs \$8 per student. At least 30 students must
  attend. There are no other costs. Describe all possible numbers of
  students who can attend without exceeding the budget. State your answer
  both as an inequality and in words.
  \vspace*{\stretch{1}}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,resume]
  \item Pump A can fill an empty tank in 6 hours, and Pump B can fill the
  same tank in 9 hours. Each pump operates at a constant rate, and no water
  leaves the tank.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item How long will the pumps take to fill an empty tank together?
    \item Starting with an empty tank, Pump A runs alone for 2 hours.
    Pump B then joins it. How much \emph{additional} time is needed to
    finish filling the tank?
  \end{enumerate}
  Express both answers in hours and minutes.
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A map has a scale of $1:25{,}000$: 1 cm on the map represents
  25,000 cm on the ground. A trail measures 7.2 cm on the map. A hiker
  walks the entire trail at a constant speed of 4.5 km/h, without stopping.
  Find the actual trail length in kilometers and the walking time in
  minutes. Use $100\text{ cm}=1\text{ m}$ and $1{,}000\text{ m}=1\text{ km}$.
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A robotics competition defines a performance score by
  \[
    Q=\frac{120n}{t(e+1)},
  \]
  where $n$ is the number of completed deliveries, $t$ is the positive
  elapsed time in minutes, and $e$ is the number of errors.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item Find the score for 30 deliveries in 5 minutes with 1 error.
    \item A robot completes 30 deliveries with 2 errors and earns a score
    of 300. How many minutes did it take?
    \item If the deliveries and time in part (a) stay the same but the
    errors increase from 1 to 3, by what factor does the score change?
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace*{\stretch{1}}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,resume]
  \item Six students record the following reading times, in minutes:
  \[
    12,\quad 14,\quad 14,\quad 16,\quad 19,\quad x.
  \]
  The mean is 17 minutes.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item Find $x$ and the median reading time.
    \item The student whose time was $x$ corrects it to 57 minutes. Find
    the new mean and median.
    \item Which measure better describes a typical reading time after
    the correction? Justify your choice using the data.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A lab needs 12 liters of a solution that is 30\% concentrate by
  volume. It has only a 20\% solution and a 35\% solution of the same
  concentrate. Assume volumes add when mixed. How many liters of each
  solution should be combined?
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A supplier charges a fixed delivery fee plus the same price for
  each kit. The table shows three complete orders; there are no taxes or
  other charges.
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{c|ccc}
      Number of kits & 2 & 5 & 9 \\
      \hline
      Total cost (dollars) & 19 & 37 & 61
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item Find the cost per kit and the delivery fee. Write a formula for
    the total cost $C$ of an order of $n$ kits.
    \item Predict the cost of 20 kits.
    \item What is the greatest number of kits that can be ordered with
    \$100, including delivery?
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace*{\stretch{1}}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,resume]
  \item Two printers work at constant rates. The faster printer takes
  3 hours less than the slower printer to complete the same printing job
  alone. Working together, they complete one such job in 2 hours.
  How long would each printer take to complete the job alone? Show why
  any rejected algebraic solution cannot describe the situation.
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item A rectangular garden measures 20 meters by 14 meters. A path of
  uniform width is built \emph{inside} the garden along all four edges,
  leaving a smaller rectangle for planting. The planting area is
  160 square meters. Find the width of the path and the dimensions of
  the planting rectangle. Explain any restriction on the path width.
  \vspace{\stretch{1}}

  \item At a price of \$20 per sweatshirt, a club expects to sell
  80 sweatshirts. For each \$1 increase in price, it expects to sell
  2 fewer sweatshirts. The club pays \$6 for each sweatshirt sold and
  has no other costs. Assume the prediction is exact. Prices may only
  increase from \$20 in whole-dollar steps, and predicted sales must
  be nonnegative.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item Let $x$ be the number of \$1 price increases. Write a formula
    for the club's total profit and state the possible values of $x$.
    \item What selling price produces the greatest profit? Find that
    profit and the predicted number of sweatshirts sold.
    \item Find all allowed selling prices for which the predicted
    profit is at least \$1,400.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace*{\stretch{1}}
\end{enumerate}
